Layout & Orientation Guide

End Gable vs. Side Gable vs. Porch Layout

Choosing the right roof orientation keeps snow shedding away from your doorway and maximizes usable wall space inside.

Best for NB Snow Shedding

End Gable (Standard)

Doors sit under the triangular gable peak so sliding roof snow sheds safely off the left and right sides—never piling up in front of your door.

Wide Driveway / Easy Access

Side Gable

Doors sit along the long wall for wide-open access to mowers and racks on both ends. Best paired with a covered overhang or gravel splash strip.

Campgrounds & Studios

4-Ft Covered Porch

Extends the main roof 4 feet over a pressure-treated deck—creating a dry entryway for muddy boots, firewood, chairs, or a weekend bunkie.

Structural Anatomy

Built for NB snow & rain-on-snow loads.

Southern New Brunswick freeze-thaw cycles and wet coastal storms destroy cheap particle-board sheds in three seasons. Every PLACED build follows a strict ground-level structural checklist:

  • Ground Contact Base: 4×4 pressure-treated skids fastened to 2×4 / 2×6 floor joists 16" on center (optional full PT floor decking for damp sites).
  • Zero-Sag Decking: 5/8" tongue-and-groove subfloor glued and ring-shank nailed so heavy mowers never deflect between joists.
  • Steel Roof Fastening Standard: Three-ribbed 29-gauge steel panels secured with gasketed screws every 24" per rib—built to shed heavy snow cleanly without wind lift.
